Mimir: the new teaching system

An interactive tour of the agreed design in docs/teaching-preferences-design.md. Design only: none of this is shipped yet.

What changes, at a glance

Click any step of a session to see what happens there.

You control the session

No timer. A session runs until you say "end session". When a question set runs out, Mimir says so and leads into the next one.

Mode from the real exam time

Urgent within 48 hours of the actual exam time. You can choose Standard anyway, or ask to cram earlier.

Three teaching settings

Support, question frequency, question depth. Global across courses, each with three levels. Urgent ignores them.

No time forecasts

No "62 days × 1–3 min". No cost of delay. Urgent ranks question types; it never cuts them for a minutes budget.

How a setting changes over time

Three ways: you ask (instant), a probe trial succeeds (slow), or the one-time "fewer questions?" offer. Wrong answers never change a setting.

What happened

    Positive signs

    Explaining reasoning willingly, or exploring a follow-up. Brief answers are neutral; being right or wrong counts for nothing either way.

    Comfortable engagement

    Needed before a probe starts. Yes: last two answers were real attempts, or the student asked a follow-up. No: recent "just tell me", repeated "idk", caps, several one-word replies, or "I'm tired".

    Moving down

    Only when you ask. If the "give the answer" help fires in two separate sessions, Mimir asks once: "Want fewer questions from now on?"

    Your words win

    "Always explain first" changes the setting at once and is never probed against. "For math…" is saved globally, and Mimir says so.

    What Mimir says

      End of a set

      That's the last question in this set. Next up: Construct a CFG (seen in 7 of 11 papers). Ready? Or say "another set on set notation", "recheck", or "end session".

      Never silent, never an automatic "Good luck". This also covers the reported Codex stall after three questions.

      The knowledge gauge

      A short check of what you already know, separate from teaching settings.

      Result

      Which topics

      Only unseen ones, highest priority first. Topics with any evidence (gap, shaky, mastered) are skipped.

      How answers count

      Fresh questions before any recipe or worked answer, recorded as solved alone. One right answer never marks a topic mastered.

      No guessing

      No gauge from the course name or general knowledge: answer keys must be checkable against your course files.
