How the heart pumps blood

The heart is two pumps side by side. The right side sends blood to the lungs; the left side sends it to the body. Step through the path of the blood.

1

Right atrium. Deoxygenated blood from the body returns through the superior and inferior venae cavae into the right atrium.

2

Right ventricle. Blood passes the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle.

3

Pulmonary trunk. The right ventricle contracts; blood passes the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary trunk, which carries it to the lungs to pick up oxygen.

4

Left atrium. Oxygenated blood returns from the lungs through the pulmonary veins into the left atrium.

5

Left ventricle. Blood passes the mitral (bicuspid) valve into the left ventricle, whose wall is the thickest of the four chambers.

6

Aorta. The left ventricle contracts; blood passes the aortic valve into the aorta and out to the body.

Simplified frontal section: the patient's right is on your left. Both atria contract together, then both ventricles; the steps follow the blood along one path.
