A Gram-negative bacterium, built layer by layer

Escherichia coli is a rod-shaped, Gram-negative bacterium. Click through to build its cell from the inside out.

1

Cytoplasm. The gel-like interior holds the nucleoid (the region with the single circular chromosome), ribosomes, and often small DNA rings called plasmids.

2

Plasma membrane (inner membrane). A phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves the cell.

3

Periplasm and peptidoglycan. A thin layer of peptidoglycan sits in the periplasmic space between the two membranes. It is far thinner than in Gram-positive bacteria, which is why Gram-negative cells do not keep the crystal violet stain.

4

Outer membrane. A second bilayer whose outer leaflet carries lipopolysaccharide (LPS); porins in it let small molecules through.

5

Capsule. Many strains add a slimy polysaccharide capsule that helps them avoid the host’s immune system.

6

Flagella and pili. Flagella spread over the surface turn like propellers to swim; the shorter, thinner pili (fimbriae) help the cell stick to surfaces.

Not to scale: the envelope layers are drawn much thicker than they are, as in textbook diagrams.
